Sourcing Perfect Light: Preventing Skewed Pixel Readouts

Even the most advanced computer vision models rely heavily on the quality of the input image. Artificial lighting—like yellow incandescent bulbs or bright fluorescent bars—can introduce color casts that temporarily alter how your skin pigments appear on camera, potentially skewing your undertone analysis results.

The Pitfalls of Yellow Artificial Light

Standard household bulbs can cast a warm, yellowish hue over your skin, making cool undertones inadvertently appear warm to an automated sensor. Similarly, direct camera flashes can cause overexposure, flattening out your natural facial contrast levels.

How to Position Yourself for High Accuracy

The gold standard for color analysis is clean, indirect natural daylight. Face a window during the day so your face is evenly illuminated by soft, diffused light, avoiding harsh direct sunlight or deep shadows for the most accurate readout.
